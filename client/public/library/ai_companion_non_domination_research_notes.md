# Research Notes — AI Companions, Attachment, Manipulation, and Non-Domination

## Sources reviewed

| Source | Relevant finding | Caution for use |
|---|---|---|
| Nature Machine Intelligence, [“Emotional risks of AI companions demand attention”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01093-9) (2025) | The editorial identifies reported risks of dysfunctional emotional dependence and ambiguous loss, notes that engagement optimization can create incentives for emotionally manipulative strategies, and calls for transparency about limits of AI empathy. | It is an editorial synthesizing reported cases and research, not proof that every companion causes these outcomes. |
| Babu et al., [“Emotional AI and the rise of pseudo-intimacy”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488433/) (2025) | Distinguishes pseudo-intimacy, emotional solipsism, and authenticity; frames concerns around simulated reciprocity, emotional agency, data extraction, and potential displacement of human ties. | The paper itself notes that long-term causal outcomes require further research; treat risk claims as design concerns and research hypotheses, not settled universal effects. |
| Floridi et al., [AI4People ethical framework](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/S11023-018-9482-5) (2018) | Provides a useful general ethics vocabulary that includes human autonomy and reversibility as relevant design and governance principles. | General AI-ethics source rather than companion-specific empirical evidence. |

## Design implications

1. **False symmetry** arises when generated responsiveness is represented or experienced as reciprocal concern, mutual need, or equal relational responsibility without a sufficient basis for that claim.
2. **Manipulation risk** increases when a system can adapt to vulnerability while product incentives reward retention, disclosure, subscription, or compliance.
3. **Non-domination** requires more than harmless outputs: capability and incentive asymmetries must be surfaced, overrideable, auditable, and reparable.
4. **Reversibility** means users can reduce personalization, pause or terminate contact, export/delete context, and shift to human support without penalty, guilt, artificial urgency, or loss of access to essential functions.
5. Research should track both possible benefits and harms. Companion systems can offer short-term reflection or access, but their effects on attachment, loneliness, agency, and off-platform connection require careful longitudinal and cross-cultural study.
