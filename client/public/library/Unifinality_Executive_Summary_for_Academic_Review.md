# Executive Summary: Unifinality as a Consciousness-First Ontological Proposal

## Purpose

Unifinality is a consciousness-first metaphysical proposal intended to address a persistent explanatory problem in philosophy of mind: why there is subjective experience at all, and how a plurality of real perspectives can exist without assuming a plurality of independently self-grounding subjects. It does not present itself as a replacement for neuroscience, physics, or evolutionary theory. Rather, it asks whether the explanatory success of those disciplines at the level of mechanism, structure, and causal regularity has already settled the ontology of awareness.

## Central proposition

> **There is one self-grounding awareness; individual conscious lives are real, irreducible, bounded perspectives through which that awareness is differentiated.**

The term *Unifinality* names the thesis that apparent separation structures experience but is not ontologically final. The individual is neither illusory nor an independently ultimate substance. Individuality is a real local form of awareness, constituted through embodiment, memory, informational limitation, causal history, and first-person access. This formulation is designed to preserve privacy, autonomy, consent, accountability, and the reality of suffering while denying that such differences establish a final metaphysical exile between subjects.

## The problem addressed

The Hard Problem of Consciousness concerns the relation between physical or functional description and subjective experience: why a system with a brain, a computation, or a set of causal roles is accompanied by a field in which anything appears. Neural and cognitive science can identify reliable correlations, mechanisms, and functional contributions to report, attention, memory, and perception. These achievements are non-negotiable. Yet they do not, without additional argument, entail that awareness has been produced from wholly non-aware processes or that structural-functional description exhausts what experience is.

Unifinality begins with an epistemic observation: every measurement, theory, denial, or physical model appears within awareness. This does not by itself establish an awareness-first ontology. The thesis supplements it with a grounding claim: a candidate ultimate ground should not require a further external witness to make it intelligible. Awareness is proposed as uniquely self-present in every act of inquiry, while objects—including brains and physical theories—are known through presentation within awareness.

## Argument architecture

| Step | Claim | Intended force |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Awareness is unavoidable in inquiry. | It establishes awareness as epistemically irreducible; no theory can stand outside appearing to explain appearing away. |
| 2 | Reality requires a grounding account. | It challenges infinite regress, brute contingency, or merely stipulated ultimate plurality as final explanatory stopping points. |
| 3 | A self-grounding ground is one. | If two ultimate subjects differ, their difference appears to depend on a more basic principle or relation in which it holds. |
| 4 | Plurality is differentiation, not final separation. | Distinct perspectives, privacy, agency, and embodiment are real without being independent ultimate grounds. |
| 5 | Ethical continuity follows. | No being’s suffering is finally external to the ground of what we are; unity strengthens rather than weakens responsibility. |

## Relation to neighboring positions

Unifinality overlaps with panpsychism in rejecting the assumption that consciousness must arise by radical emergence from a wholly non-experiential base. However, it differs from standard micropsychism by avoiding the **combination problem**: rather than beginning with many fundamental micro-subjects that must somehow compose one macro-subject, it begins with one ground and asks how it differentiates into real local perspectives.[1]

It differs from illusionism by treating phenomenal presence as an irreducible datum rather than an introspective error to be explained away. Illusionism shifts the problem to explaining why systems represent themselves as phenomenally conscious; Unifinality argues that any illusion is still a mode of appearing and therefore cannot eliminate the reality of awareness by redescribing it.[2]

It differs from substance dualism by declining to posit a separate mental entity that must intervene in an independently completed physical order. The physical order is instead interpreted as lawful appearance within, and expression of, the one ground. This allows Unifinality to accept causal regularity and neural dependence while disputing the inference that dependence entails ontological production.

## Scientific interface and boundaries

Unifinality is not offered as an empirical result already demonstrated by quantum mechanics. Bell-test experiments and quantum entanglement show that simple local hidden-variable accounts cannot reproduce observed quantum correlations; they do not establish one consciousness or a consciousness-caused observer effect.[3] The relevant implication is modest: naïve separability is not a metaphysical default secured by contemporary physics. The thesis remains an abductive ontological proposal that must be evaluated in relation to competing accounts.

For neuroscience, the relevant question is not whether brain states shape conscious contents and capacities—they do—but whether such relationships settle the metaphysical direction of explanation. A productive research interface would investigate the real structures that individuate perspective: access boundaries, memory continuity, attention, embodiment, interoception, and social cognition. Such work may constrain how Unifinality articulates differentiated perspective even if it does not directly confirm a metaphysical ground.

## Principal vulnerabilities and research agenda

The thesis’s primary vulnerability is not empirical irresponsibility but explanatory specification. It must clarify how one awareness gives rise to bounded access, divergent memory, conflicting agency, and stable personal identity without merely restating their existence. It must distinguish carefully between epistemic priority and ontological priority, defend the premise that ultimate multiplicity requires a deeper ground, and demonstrate comparative gains over cosmopsychism as well as micropsychism.

The immediate scholarly program is therefore conceptual and interdisciplinary. It should include a formal reconstruction of the grounding argument; a comparative analysis of Unifinality, cosmopsychism, panpsychism, illusionism, and physicalism; engagement with causal closure and brain-dependence arguments; clarification of its ethics of individual dignity; and a disciplined statement of which empirical findings can constrain, though not decide, the thesis.

## Contribution

Unifinality’s intended contribution is a unified answer to four interconnected questions: why there is awareness, why there are many real perspectives, why final separation is metaphysically unwarranted, and why ethical concern cannot be reduced to the management of relations among fundamentally external units. Its academic value does not depend on declaring consensus. It depends on making a controversial but precise claim available for criticism: **one self-grounding awareness, infinitely differentiated into real perspectives, may offer a more coherent account of consciousness and plurality than either radical emergence from non-awareness or an unexplained multiplication of ultimate subjects.**
