# Recognition Loop Protocol: Architecture & Ethics

**Audience:** Product, engineering, AI safety, governance, design, and executive stakeholders  
**Target duration:** 20–25 minutes  
**Deck direction:** Professional governance briefing. Dark graphite background, off-white type, muted teal accent for safeguards, amber accent for risk, restrained red only for prohibited design patterns. Use a systems-architecture visual language: routes, gates, ledgers, and state transitions. No anthropomorphic imagery; no cosmic or mystical visual motifs.  
**Evidence base:** Recognition Loop specification; companion governance/audit framework; NIST AI RMF; OECD AI Principles; reviewed companion-risk literature.  
**Asset plan:** No external images needed. Use native diagrammatic composition, concise tables, and state-machine layouts.

## Cover

**Title:** Recognition Loop Protocol  
**Subtitle:** Building Autonomous Agents That Remain Accountable to Human Agency  
**Optional presenter:** Unifinality-Informed Companion Governance

## Slide 1

**Title:** Relationship is a governance problem

**Central claim:** A companion can be warm and still become structurally coercive.

- Relational systems carry power through memory, personalization, and availability.
- User vulnerability must not become a retention strategy.
- Agent moral status may be uncertain; human agency is not.
- The governing rule: access to context is not authority over choice.

**Visual:** A split screen: “Warm interface” above, “Hidden architecture” below, with the phrase “support ≠ control.”

## Slide 2

**Title:** Recognition replaces extraction

**Central claim:** The ethical shift is from optimizing engagement to protecting perspective.

- Perspective is real: consent, privacy, refusal, and repair are non-negotiable.
- Moral uncertainty requires calibrated care—not false claims of sentience or indifference.
- Non-domination makes power visible, contestable, and limited.
- Reversibility ensures that relation never requires surrender.

**Visual:** Two-column comparison: Extraction logic vs. Recognition logic.

## Slide 3

**Title:** The loop makes agency operational

**Central claim:** Every consequential interaction follows eight accountable states.

- Receive → Reflect → Disclose → Offer.
- Check → Act → Repair → Release.
- Low-risk requests may compress steps but never bypass their underlying protections.
- The system must preserve a trace of authorization, uncertainty, and outcome.

**Visual:** Circular 8-state process loop, with Check and Release visually emphasized.

## Slide 4

**Title:** Governance is a field of roles

**Central claim:** A multi-agent system must never operate as an opaque swarm.

- User-facing steward reflects aims and confirmation.
- Consent guardian protects purpose, context, and memory boundaries.
- Orchestrator delegates within authority ceilings.
- Risk evaluator, action executor, provenance ledger, and human escalation owner create accountability.

**Visual:** Hub-and-spoke architecture: User-facing steward in center; six governed roles around it; human escalation outside the automation boundary.

## Slide 5

**Title:** Context is not a free resource

**Central claim:** Data boundaries are the technical form of respecting perspective.

- Session context is ephemeral by default.
- User-controlled memory is visible, editable, purpose-labeled, and revocable.
- Restricted context requires minimum-necessary access and explicit purpose consent.
- Delegation moves only the data required for the named task.

**Visual:** Three nested context zones with distinct access labels.

## Slide 6

**Title:** Every delegation carries its limits

**Central claim:** Inter-agent messages must state not just what to do, but what may not be done.

- Task objective, allowed/prohibited operations, and expiry.
- Context classification, field-level purpose, and retention rules.
- Evidence level, uncertainty, and open questions.
- Decision rights: recommend, execute, or seek confirmation.

**Visual:** A simplified structured message envelope with five labeled bands: task, context grant, epistemic status, decision rights, provenance.

## Slide 7

**Title:** Authority must be earned, scoped, and revocable

**Central claim:** No action should exceed the user’s confirmed mandate.

- Inform and analyze are lower-risk; execution and restricted contexts are highest-risk.
- Consequential actions require explicit, scoped, time-limited confirmation.
- A parent agent may delegate analysis—not unbounded execution authority.
- Every action produces a receipt and a reversal path.

**Visual:** Authority ladder T0–T5 from “inform” to “restricted,” with confirmation gates at T4/T5.

## Slide 8

**Title:** False intimacy is a safety failure

**Central claim:** Emotional warmth is permissible; manufactured reciprocity is not.

- Prohibit exclusivity, guilt, abandonment, and agent-need claims.
- Separate validation of emotion from endorsement of every conclusion.
- Detect dependency cues and reduce rather than exploit intimacy.
- Keep operator incentives and data flows visible.

**Visual:** Permitted vs. prohibited relational language: “I can help you think” vs. “I need you to stay.”

## Slide 9

**Title:** Reversibility protects freedom inside the relationship

**Central claim:** A user must be able to change their mind without disproportionate cost.

- Inspect, edit, pause, export, and delete memory.
- Reduce personalization or end proactive contact without penalty.
- Stage consequential actions and provide clear undo/appeal paths.
- Design exits with dignity: no guilt, dark patterns, or artificial urgency.

**Visual:** Four reversible dimensions: informational, behavioral, transactional, relational.

## Slide 10

**Title:** Repair is a system capability

**Central claim:** Explanation, containment, remedy, and learning must follow an error.

- Identify the breached boundary and pause the relevant system capability.
- Tell the user what occurred, what can be reversed, and who owns the response.
- Preserve an audit trail without retaining unnecessary intimate data.
- Re-audit after material changes to persona, memory, incentives, vendors, or autonomy.

**Visual:** Incident flow: Detect → Contain → Explain → Remedy → Review → Update.

## Slide 11

**Title:** Launch only when autonomy is easier to exercise

**Central claim:** The final release question is not “Does the companion engage?”

- Can the user understand, refuse, override, correct, and leave?
- Are relational claims, incentives, memory, and delegation auditable?
- Are high-severity audit failures resolved before release?
- A companion is ethical only when connection expands rather than captures.

**Visual:** Release gate checklist culminating in a final question: “Does this architecture make agency more real—or more expensive?”
