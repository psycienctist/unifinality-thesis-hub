## Comparison anchors

1. The **Hard Problem** is the problem of why physical or functional processes are accompanied by subjective experience—the fact that there is something it is like for an organism or system to be in a state.
2. **Panpsychism** avoids strong emergence by locating mentality in the fundamental constituents or ground of nature, but standard micropsychist variants inherit the combination and subject-summing problems: how many micro-subjects could compose a unified macro-subject.
3. **Illusionism** rejects or revises the target: phenomenal consciousness, understood as private, intrinsic, ineffable qualitative properties, is said to be misrepresented by introspection. The explanatory burden shifts to why consciousness seems to have those properties.
4. **Unifinality**, as supplied in the user’s thesis, locates awareness as one self-grounding ground prior to all object-level inquiry. It proposes differentiation rather than aggregation: individual perspectives are real local expressions of one ground, so it avoids the need to combine many independently fundamental micro-subjects.
5. The strongest discipline for a scientific audience is to state that Unifinality is **metaphysical and explanatory**, not a result already established by neuroscience, quantum mechanics, or Bell tests.
