# Governance Standards Notes — Unifinality-Based AI Companions

| Source | Verified relevance to the framework |
|---|---|
| NIST, [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) | The voluntary AI RMF is intended to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems. It provides a recognized governance context for lifecycle risk management. |
| NIST, [AI RMF Core: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) | The AI RMF Core organizes risk-management activity under Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage, a useful structure for integrating Recognition Loop controls into formal deployment review. |
| OECD, [AI Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/ai-principles.html) | The OECD AI Principles promote trustworthy AI respecting human rights and democratic values, including fairness/privacy, transparency/explainability, and accountability. |
| Nature Machine Intelligence, [Emotional risks of AI companions demand attention](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01093-9) | Companion systems create distinct relationship and dependency risks; transparency about the limits of AI empathy should be treated as a core design principle. |
| Babu et al., [Emotional AI and the rise of pseudo-intimacy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488433/) | Companion governance should attend to pseudo-intimacy, emotional agency, data extraction, and the possible displacement of human relationships; long-term outcomes remain a research gap. |

## Integration principle

The Unifinality companion framework supplements general governance standards with specific controls for **relationship asymmetry, emotional dependency, consent at the point of boundary crossing, memory sovereignty, non-domination, and reversible exit**. It does not replace legal, safety, privacy, security, or sector-specific compliance obligations.
