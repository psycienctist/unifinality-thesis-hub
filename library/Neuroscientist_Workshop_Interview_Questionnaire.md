# Neuroscientist Interview Questionnaire for the Unifinality Workshop

## Purpose and use

This semi-structured questionnaire is designed for neuroscientists participating in the Unifinality multidisciplinary workshop. It is not a belief survey and should not be used to solicit endorsement. Its purpose is to identify where the thesis is **conceptually clear, scientifically constrained, vulnerable, or potentially generative** for research on consciousness.

> **Neutral opening statement for the interviewer:** “Unifinality is a metaphysical proposal that treats awareness as one self-grounding ground differentiated into many real perspectives. It does not claim that neuroscience is false, that brains are irrelevant, or that quantum mechanics has experimentally proved one consciousness. We are interested in your critical assessment of its assumptions, its possible points of contact with neuroscience, and the standards it would need to meet to earn serious engagement.”

## Administration profile

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Format** | One-to-one semi-structured interview, in person or video call |
| **Target duration** | 45–60 minutes |
| **Interviewer stance** | Curious, non-defensive, and non-leading; request examples and distinguish evidence from metaphysical inference |
| **Recording** | Obtain explicit consent before audio/video recording or taking identifiable notes |
| **Reporting** | Attribute only with written permission; otherwise de-identify comments by broad discipline and career stage |
| **Materials provided in advance** | One-page Unifinality brief, a glossary, and the physics-boundary note |

## Participant profile

### A. Background and orientation — 5 minutes

1. Could you briefly describe your primary research area, methods, and the kinds of consciousness-related questions your work engages?

2. Which explanatory level do you work at most often: cellular/molecular mechanisms, circuits and networks, systems neuroscience, computational modeling, behavior, clinical neuroscience, or another level?

3. How would you describe your current working view of consciousness: reductive physicalist, functionalist, emergentist, neutral monist, panpsychist, agnostic, or another position? What leads you to that view?

4. In your own words, what would count as a successful scientific explanation of consciousness?

**Optional rating:** On a scale from 1 to 7, where **1** means “consciousness is fully explainable by present physical-functional concepts” and **7** means “consciousness likely requires a revision of our basic ontology,” where would you currently place yourself? What motivates that rating?

## The empirical architecture of conscious perspective

### B. What neuroscience has established — 8 minutes

5. Which findings most strongly support the claim that neural organization conditions the contents, capacities, or continuity of conscious experience?

6. In your judgment, what follows from those findings at each of the following levels: **correlation**, **causal intervention**, **functional contribution**, **constitutive identity**, and **ontological production**?

7. Are there places where public discussion too quickly moves from a legitimate neural finding to a stronger metaphysical conclusion? Could you give an example?

8. How should a theory of consciousness accommodate anesthesia, lesions, pharmacology, brain stimulation, developmental variation, and altered states without dismissing their evidentiary weight?

**Interviewer follow-up:** “Does the evidence require that the brain produces awareness, or does it require only that the brain structures and conditions human conscious experience? Please explain the distinction if you think it is meaningful.”

## The Hard Problem and explanatory sufficiency

### C. What remains after mechanisms are mapped — 8 minutes

9. Do you regard the Hard Problem—the question of why physical or functional processes are accompanied by subjective experience—as a genuine problem, a temporary explanatory gap, a conceptual confusion, or something else?

10. Suppose we had a complete neural and computational account of a subject’s discriminations, reports, attention, memory, behavior, and self-modeling. What, if anything, would remain unexplained about first-person experience?

11. Is the phrase “there is something it is like” scientifically useful, scientifically neutral, or conceptually misleading? Why?

12. In your field, which measures are most informative about consciousness, and what do they leave underdetermined—report, behavior, neural complexity, perturbational measures, functional connectivity, or other metrics?

**Optional rating:** On a scale from 1 to 7, how confident are you that a complete account of conscious experience can be stated entirely in structural-functional terms? What would make you revise that confidence?

## Unifinality: clarity and pressure points

### D. Response to the thesis — 12 minutes

13. Having read the brief, what do you take Unifinality to claim in its strongest and most charitable form?

14. Which part of the thesis do you find most coherent or potentially valuable: awareness’s irreducibility in inquiry, the grounding argument, the one-ground claim, differentiated perspective, or its ethical implications? Why?

15. Which premise is least convincing or most underdefined? What would a more rigorous formulation require?

16. Unifinality distinguishes **empirical dependence** from **ontological production**: brain states may condition the contents and capacities of experience without thereby proving that awareness is produced from non-awareness. Is this distinction coherent? If not, where exactly does it fail?

17. The thesis proposes that individual perspectives are real local differentiations of one ground rather than independently self-grounding subjects. What empirical or conceptual phenomena must it explain particularly well—privacy, memory boundaries, agency, dissociation, split-brain findings, social cognition, or something else?

18. Does Unifinality offer explanatory content beyond relabeling consciousness as “one awareness”? What would it need to add to make that content more explicit?

**Interviewer follow-up:** “Please formulate the strongest neuroscientific objection you would raise to the thesis, in the language you would use in a peer-review report.”

## Individuality, boundaries, and ethics

### E. Distinguishing unity from erasure — 5 minutes

19. What neural, bodily, and social processes do you regard as central to the formation and maintenance of a first-person boundary?

20. Could a shared-ground metaphysics coexist with the empirical reality of private access, autonomous agency, consent, responsibility, and divergent memory? What would the thesis have to say to make this credible?

21. What ethical risks arise if a unity thesis is stated carelessly? What safeguards should be explicit in any Unifinality research program?

## Research-program design

### F. From metaphysics to constrained inquiry — 10 minutes

22. In your view, can a metaphysical thesis be scientifically productive without directly functioning as an empirical theory? Under what conditions?

23. Which research questions could genuinely constrain or refine Unifinality’s account of *differentiated perspective* without falsely claiming to test a universal ground directly?

24. Which existing domains might be most informative: neural correlates of conscious access, disorders of consciousness, split-brain research, anesthesia, development, interoception, psychedelic states, multisensory integration, social neuroscience, consciousness in nonhuman animals, or AI consciousness? Why?

25. What would a formal or computational model of differentiated perspective need to specify for you to take it seriously?

26. What empirical result, conceptual argument, or internal inconsistency would most strongly count **against** Unifinality’s current formulation?

27. What result or theoretical development would make you more willing to engage the thesis as a serious interdisciplinary proposal?

## Closing reflection

### G. Final questions — 5 minutes

28. What is the most important correction you would make to how Unifinality is currently presented to a neuroscience audience?

29. What is one question the workshop must not avoid if it wants to earn scientific respect?

30. Is there a way your own research program might contribute a useful constraint, method, or conceptual distinction to this discussion?

31. May we contact you after the workshop to clarify your comments, invite a written critique, or explore an adversarial-collaboration session?

## Interviewer coding guide

Use the coding guide after the interview, not while the participant is speaking. The aim is to preserve nuance rather than force responses into a pro/con binary.

| Code | Description | Sample indicator |
|---|---|---|
| **ND** | Neural dependence recognized | “This manipulation reliably changes conscious report.” |
| **PI** | Physicalist/identity inference | “The best explanation is that consciousness is brain activity.” |
| **FG** | Functional or computational grounding | “The relevant issue is information-processing role.” |
| **HP** | Hard Problem accepted as substantive | “A complete neural model may still leave qualitative presence open.” |
| **IL** | Illusionist or deflationary move | “The sense of ineffable phenomenality may be a cognitive construction.” |
| **UM** | Unity metaphysics considered coherent | “Privacy does not logically entail separate ultimate subjects.” |
| **IN** | Individuation objection | “The view needs a stronger account of memory and agency boundaries.” |
| **EP** | Empirical-program requirement | “Specify what evidence would discriminate this from rivals.” |
| **ET** | Ethical concern or safeguard | “Unity must not undermine autonomy or consent.” |
| **CO** | Collaboration opportunity | “My lab/method could test or constrain this component.” |

## Facilitation note

Do not treat agreement as success. A high-value interview may end with a sophisticated objection that forces a sharper thesis. In this workshop, the desired outcome is a more rigorous articulation of the relation between **brain-conditioned perspective** and **awareness as proposed ground**, not the premature conversion of a neuroscientist to a metaphysical conclusion.
