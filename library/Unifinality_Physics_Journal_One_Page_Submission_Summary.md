# Unifinality, Quantum Foundations, and Consciousness: A Metaphysical Proposal Constrained by Physics

**Article type:** Conceptual / foundations perspective  
**Keywords:** consciousness; quantum foundations; measurement problem; metaphysics; separability; individuation; philosophy of physics

## Abstract

**Unifinality** is a consciousness-first metaphysical proposal: reality is ultimately grounded in one self-grounding awareness, expressed through many real, bounded, and irreducible perspectives. This submission does not offer a modification of quantum dynamics, a theory of consciousness-caused wave-function collapse, or a claim that quantum mechanics has empirically demonstrated a universal subject. Rather, it asks whether the existence of awareness and the unity of conscious perspective are adequately grounded by ontologies that treat experience as emergent from a wholly non-experiential basis or as belonging to many independently ultimate subjects.

The article’s physics-facing contribution is deliberately limited. Quantum measurement and Bell-test results do not establish Unifinality. Bell-type experiments constrain classes of simple local hidden-variable accounts under their relevant assumptions; they do not prove consciousness, numerical identity of minds, or mental causation of outcomes.[1] Likewise, environmental decoherence accounts for the suppression of interference through system–environment interaction and supports the emergence of robust quasi-classical behavior, while the account of outcome definiteness remains interpretation-sensitive.[2] These results therefore do not validate a unity metaphysics. They do, however, make it methodologically inappropriate to treat naïve local separability as a settled metaphysical baseline.

## Central thesis

The article advances four linked claims. First, awareness is **epistemically irreducible**: every measurement, physical model, and denial of consciousness is presented in awareness. This premise does not automatically yield ontological priority. Second, the article argues that an ultimate explanatory ground cannot derive its intelligibility from an external witness; awareness is proposed as a candidate for such a self-present ground. Third, it argues that a plurality of independently self-grounding subjects requires a principled account of the differentiation that makes them ultimately distinct. Fourth, it proposes that the plurality of persons is real as **differentiated perspective**—with genuine boundaries of access, memory, embodiment, agency, and ethical standing—without entailing final metaphysical separation.

## Relation to quantum theory

The article is interpretation-neutral. It explicitly distinguishes the measurement problem, state-update rules, literal physical collapse in objective-collapse models, Bohmian dynamics, Everettian unitary accounts, and Copenhagen-family approaches. “Observer” is used only for measurement context—system, apparatus, environmental coupling, record, and protocol—not as a synonym for a conscious human mind.[3] No causal role is assigned to awareness within quantum dynamics. If a future Unifinality-inspired physical model were proposed, it would require a specified state space, dynamics, operational variable, coupling law, quantitative predictions, and falsifiers relative to existing approaches.[4]

## Contribution and review criteria

The submission’s value lies in making a disputed metaphysical question explicit while preserving the authority of physics over physical claims. It invites review on whether: **(i)** the transition from awareness’s epistemic irreducibility to a grounding claim is valid; **(ii)** the one-ground argument avoids merely asserting unity; **(iii)** differentiated perspective can explain privacy, agency, and intersubjective disagreement; and **(iv)** the proposal has explanatory advantages over physicalism, panpsychism, cosmopsychism, idealism, or neutral monism. The thesis should be rejected if it equivocates between these levels, relies on quantum overclaim, or fails to state a coherent account of individuation.
